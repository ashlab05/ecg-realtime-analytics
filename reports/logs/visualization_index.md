## 3. Anomaly Detection & Explainability Figures

### Figure 9: Reconstruction Error Distribution & Threshold
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/reconstruction_error_distribution.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/reconstruction_error_distribution.pdf`
- **Description:** Overlaid probability density histograms of Normal beats ($N=13,513$) versus Abnormal beats ($N=1,591$) with the calibrated decision threshold ($0.0163$ for GWO-AE).
- **Data Source:** Reconstruction errors from test predictions.
- **Key Observation:** The Normal distribution is tightly concentrated near zero, whereas arrhythmic beats exhibit a long right-tail of reconstruction failure.

### Figure 10: Representative Normal vs. Arrhythmic Feature Reconstructions
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/representative_feature_reconstructions.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/representative_feature_reconstructions.pdf`
- **Description:** Feature profiles (32 features across Lead II and Lead V) showing original standardized values vs. model reconstructions for a normal sinus beat (top) and an arrhythmic ventricular ectopic beat (bottom).
- **Data Source:** `X_test_scaled` and model `reconstruct()` outputs.
- **Key Observation:** For the normal beat, the autoencoder reconstructs feature values accurately ($MSE = 0.0039$). For the VEB sample, morphological features (e.g., QRS interval, R-peak, T-peak) diverge drastically ($MSE = 0.2814$).

### Figure 11: Feature-Level Reconstruction Error for Anomalies
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/feature_level_error_anomalies.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/feature_level_error_anomalies.pdf`
- **Description:** Per-feature mean squared error comparing normal beats versus abnormal beats across all 32 canonical features.
- **Data Source:** Feature-wise squared error computed on test set.
- **Key Observation:** Errors in abnormal beats are heavily concentrated in RR-intervals (`pre-RR`, `post-RR`), QRS intervals, and morphological descriptors (`qrs_morph0`–`qrs_morph2`).

### Figure 12: Top 10 Diagnostic Features Driving Anomaly Gating
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/top_contributing_features.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/top_contributing_features.pdf`
- **Description:** Horizontal bar chart ranking the top 10 features with the highest reconstruction error discrepancy between abnormal and normal heartbeats.
- **Data Source:** Per-feature $\Delta MSE = MSE_{abnormal} - MSE_{normal}$.
- **Key Observation:** `Pre-RR Interval (Lead II)`, `Post-RR Interval (Lead II)`, `QRS Duration (Lead II)`, and `QRS Morph 1 (Lead V)` are the primary drivers of anomaly gating decisions.

### Figure 13: Threshold Sensitivity Analysis
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/threshold_sensitivity.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/threshold_sensitivity.pdf`
- **Description:** Curve of F1-Score, Precision, and Recall across normal validation calibration percentiles from 80% to 99.5%.
- **Data Source:** Calibration sweep on normal validation errors applied to held-out test set.
- **Key Observation:** The $p_{95}$ calibration policy strikes an optimal balance, yielding near-peak F1 while preventing excessive false positive alarms.

## 4. Robustness & Streaming Scalability Figures

### Figure 14: Model Robustness Under Feature Noise
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/noise_robustness_analysis.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/noise_robustness_analysis.pdf`
- **Description:** Degradation of Test AUROC and F1-Score under increasing levels of additive Gaussian noise $\sigma \in [0.0, 0.50]$.
- **Data Source:** Controlled noise injection sweep on test feature vectors.
- **Key Observation:** Because GWO and PSO were trained with internal denoising corruption, they maintain AUROC $>0.88$ even under severe noise ($\sigma = 0.25$).

### Figure 15: Distribution Drift Simulation
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/distribution_drift_analysis.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/distribution_drift_analysis.pdf`
- **Description:** Sensitivity of test AUROC and False Positive Rate under simulated baseline mean drift on Lead II features ($\Delta \mu \in [0.0, 1.0]$).
- **Data Source:** Controlled feature mean shift simulation.
- **Key Observation:** Illustrates the vulnerability of fixed-threshold autoencoders to sensor drift, underscoring the clinical necessity of dynamic baseline recalibration in long-term ambulatory streaming.

### Figure 16: Inference Latency & Throughput Scaling
- **File Paths:**
  - PNG: `reports/figures/streaming_throughput_latency.png`
  - PDF: `reports/figures/streaming_throughput_latency.pdf`
- **Description:** Dual-axis log-scale plot showing per-beat inference latency ($\mu s$) and throughput ($k$ events/s) across batch sizes $B \in [1, 4096]$.
- **Data Source:** Timed execution on held-out test data.
- **Key Observation:** Per-sample latency drops from $14.2 \mu s$ at single-sample streaming ($B=1$) to $0.55 \mu s$ at batch $B=512$, corresponding to throughput scaling from 70k to 1.9M events/second.
